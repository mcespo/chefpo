## Masala Chai

### Description

I used to have a bad habit of calling this chai tea (which would be the equivalent of me saying tea-tea). In any case, masala chai is pretty stellar if you make it on your own. General recipes only call for ginger, cardamom, cloves, and cinnamon, while others also use pepper, mint, etc.

Consider masala chai a house specialty. You will have versions that you like and others that you don't. The beauty is that adding or removing spices, water, steeping the tea for longer — literally any change you make will turn this into _your_ version of masala chai. So don't feel pressured to procure spices you would not normally own. Know that I am inviting you to my version below. So let's get to business. ☕

**Yield:** 800–900ml, about 4–6 cups

### Recipe

<Steps>
1. **Prep the spices:** Chop or grate the ginger. Crush some of the cinnamon with the side of a knife — just damage it safely. In a mortar and pestle, press half of the cardamom, half of the peppercorns, and half of the allspice. Don't turn this into a powder, just give it a good few little grinds.

2. **Boil the spices:** To a pot, add the 600g of water, all of the ginger, cardamom, cloves, fennel, cinnamon, peppercorns, and allspice, and grate a pinch of nutmeg over the water. Bring to a boil before moving on.

3. **Add the tea:** As the water starts to boil, add the 9g of tea.

4. **Add the milk:** After 1 minute, add the 300g of milk.

5. **Taste:** Let it sit for a few minutes and taste for spice. If all checks out, add sugar conservatively, to taste.

6. **Strain or press:** Either strain into a container or transfer to a French press (my preferred method). If using a French press, transfer all of the liquid but avoid transferring too many of the tea leaves — they'll change the taste if left in too long (and they won't fit in the press anyway).
</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
  The 800–900ml yield has a wide margin because it depends on whether you strain out all the bits or keep some of them in a large container.
</Aside>
