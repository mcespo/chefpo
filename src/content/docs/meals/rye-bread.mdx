## Rye Bread, The Recipe Grinder

### Description

A no-knead rye bread with a long, slow first rise that develops flavor overnight. The dough goes straight into a screaming-hot covered pot to create a crispy crust and open crumb.

**Total time:** 14–21 hours (mostly hands-off)

### Ingredients

| Item                  | Amount             |
| --------------------- | ------------------ |
| bread flour           | 250g               |
| rye flour             | 150g + for dusting |
| table salt            | 7g (1¼ tsp)        |
| instant or active dry yeast | 7g           |
| cool water (55–65°F)  | 380g               |

### Tools

| Items                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------- |
| large mixing bowl                              |
| bowl scraper or rubber spatula                 |
| plastic wrap                                   |
| tea towel                                      |
| 4½–5½ quart heavy covered pot (dutch oven)     |
| oven                                           |
| wire rack                                      |
| potholders                                     |

### Recipe

<Steps>
1. In a medium bowl, stir together the bread flour, rye flour, salt, and yeast. Add the water and mix with a wooden spoon or your hand until you have a wet, sticky dough, about 30 seconds.

2. Cover the bowl with plastic wrap and let sit at room temperature until the surface is dotted with bubbles and the dough has more than doubled in size, **12 to 18 hours**.

3. Once the first rise is complete, generously dust a work surface with flour. Scrape the dough out of the bowl in one piece using a bowl scraper or spatula.

4. With lightly floured hands, lift the edges of the dough in toward the center, nudging and tucking to form a rough round shape.

5. Generously dust a tea towel with rye flour. Place the dough on the towel seam side down. Dust the top lightly with rye flour if tacky, then fold the towel loosely over the dough. Let rise in a warm, draft-free spot for **1 to 2 hours**, until almost doubled.

6. About 30 minutes before the second rise is done, preheat the oven to **475°F (245°C)** with a rack in the lower third. Place the covered pot on the rack to preheat as well.

7. Using potholders, carefully remove the hot pot and uncover it. Unfold the towel and quickly but gently invert the dough into the pot seam side up. Cover and return to the oven.

8. Bake covered for **30 minutes**, then remove the lid and continue baking for **15 to 30 minutes** more until the crust is a deep chestnut color.

9. Lift the bread out of the pot and place on a wire rack to cool for at least **1 hour** before slicing.
</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
  In cold weather the first rise may take up to 24 hours. The dough is ready
  when the surface is bubbly and visibly more than doubled. For the second rise,
  poke the dough gently — if the indent holds, it's ready; if it springs back,
  give it another 15 minutes.
</Aside>
