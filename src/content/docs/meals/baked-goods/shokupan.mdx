### Description

Based on the recipe by Andrea Geary at [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/14709-shokupan-japanese-white-bread). A light, fluffy Japanese milk bread that naturally stays fresh longer, made with the yudane method — a boiling-water-and-flour paste that pre-gelatinizes some of the starch. Shaping the dough into snug, tension-building coils and baking in a lidded Pullman pan gives the loaf a fine crumb and perfectly square sides.

**Yield:** One 13-inch loaf · **Total time:** 1¾ hours, plus 5½ hours resting, rising, and cooling

### Recipe

<Steps>
1. **Make the yudane:** Mix the 117g of flour and 114g of freshly boiled water in the bowl of a stand mixer to form a rough but uniform dough. Once it's cool enough to handle, lightly grease your hands and tear the yudane into roughly 1-inch pieces.

2. **Hydrate:** Add the milk to the yudane and stir to combine — it will stay lumpy. Add the 416g of flour and the yeast. Fit the mixer with the dough hook and mix on low until all the flour is moistened, 1–2 minutes. Let stand for 15 minutes.

3. **Knead:** Add the sugar and salt and mix on medium-low for 10 minutes, scraping down the bowl and hook halfway through. With the mixer running, add the butter. Keep mixing on medium-low, scraping as needed, until the dough clears the sides of the bowl, 5–10 minutes longer. If it clears the sides early, keep mixing for at least the minimum time to build structure.

4. **First rise:** Transfer to a lightly oiled counter, knead briefly into a ball, and place seam side down in a lightly greased bowl. Cover and let rise until almost doubled, about 1 hour.

5. **Divide and rest:** Divide the dough into 4 equal portions. Shape into balls, place seam side down on the counter, cover, and rest for 15 minutes.

6. **Shape the coils:** Lightly oil the counter, your hands, and the rolling pin. Turn one ball seam side up and press into a 5-inch square. Roll into a 7-inch square, pressing out all the air bubbles — use the dough's tackiness and your hands to hold the shape. Fold lengthwise into thirds to form a 7-inch strip, then roll into a 9 x 3-inch strip. Starting from a short side, roll into a snug cylinder and pinch the seam to seal. Place seam side down. Repeat with the remaining balls.

7. **Pan and proof:** Spray the Pullman pan and lid with oil spray (not needed with the USA Pan). Place the cylinders seam side down in the pan, spirals facing the long sides, spaced evenly. Slide the lid on, leaving the last inch open so you can watch the rise, and cover that gap with plastic wrap. Let rise until the dough is ½ inch from the top of the pan, 1–1¼ hours.

8. **Bake:** Remove the plastic and slide the lid fully closed. Adjust an oven rack to the middle position and heat the oven to 375°F (190°C) — the loaf keeps rising while the oven heats, so wait at least 15 minutes after closing the lid before baking. Bake until the bread registers 195–200°F (91–93°C) inside, 30–35 minutes.

9. **Cool:** Remove the lid, turn the bread out onto a wire rack, then flip it upright. Let cool completely, at least 3 hours, before slicing with a serrated knife — saw gently, using very little downward pressure.
</Steps>

<Aside type="note">
  **Why the yudane works:** Equal weights of boiling water and bread flour pre-gelatinize some of the starch, which keeps the loaf soft and fresh longer. Tearing the yudane into the milk cools the yudane and warms the milk, making both friendlier to the yeast. Rolling out each portion removes air pockets, and the tight coils build structure for a fine, even crumb.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip">
  **Weigh the flour** for accuracy, and use water from a freshly boiled kettle. **No Pullman pan?** ATK's "Mountain Bread" variation bakes the dough in 2 conventional loaf pans instead.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip">
  **Storage:** Wrap and store at room temperature for up to 3 days.
</Aside>
