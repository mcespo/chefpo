### Description

Based on the recipe by [Joshua Weissman](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jfeOoyOvlpU) ("Easy Homemade English Muffins Without Special Tools"). Making English muffins takes a little patience for the proofing, but the execution is straightforward and incredibly rewarding. Instead of baking them in an oven, these bad boys get cooked right on a stovetop griddle or cast-iron skillet.

**Yield:** 9–12 muffins · **Total time:** about 2½ hours (mostly proofing)

### Ingredients

#### Liquid & Yeast

| Item             | Amount                  |
| ---------------- | ----------------------- |
| water            | 139g (½ cup + 2 tbsp)   |
| whole milk       | 139g (½ cup + 2 tbsp)   |
| active dry yeast | 9g (1 tbsp)             |

#### Dough

| Item                                     | Amount        | Notes                                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| bread flour                              | 480g (4 cups) |                                        |
| fine sea salt                            | 6g (1 tsp)    |                                        |
| granulated sugar                         | 13g (1 tbsp)  |                                        |
| unsalted butter, melted & warm           | 28g (2 tbsp)  | or canola oil                          |
| cornmeal (or all-purpose flour)          | as needed     | for dusting                            |

| rolling pin                        |

### Recipe

<Steps>
1. **Bloom the yeast:** Combine the 139g of water and 139g of milk in a heatproof container and warm to 95–100°F (35–37°C). Stir in the 9g of yeast and let it rest for 10 minutes, until hydrated and bubbly.

2. **Mix:** In a medium bowl, whisk together the 480g of flour, the salt, and the sugar. Add the bloomed yeast mixture and the melted butter — make sure the butter is just warm to the touch so it doesn't kill the yeast. Mix with your hands until a shaggy dough forms, then turn it out onto a clean work surface.

3. **Knead:** Knead for 5–10 minutes, until smooth.

4. **First rise:** Shape the dough into a smooth ball, transfer to a lightly greased bowl, cover with a damp towel, and let rise at room temperature for about 1 hour, until doubled in size.

5. **Roll & cut:** Punch the dough down. Lightly flour your work surface and rolling pin, then roll the dough out to about ½ inch thick. Cut out rounds with the 3-inch cutter. Re-roll the scraps to cut extra rounds — you should get 9–12 muffins.

6. **Second proof:** Dust a baking sheet generously with cornmeal. Place the rounds on the sheet and cover with an upside-down baking sheet or a damp towel. Let rise at room temperature for 30–60 minutes, until plump and puffed up.

7. **Cook:** Heat a cast-iron skillet or griddle over medium heat and lightly grease it. Add the rounds, leaving at least ½ inch between them so they aren't crowded. Cook for 5–7 minutes per side, until golden brown. Lower the heat if they start browning too quickly.

8. **Cool:** Transfer to a wire rack and let cool completely.
</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
  **Keep the liquids at 95–100°F (35–37°C).** That's warm enough to wake up the yeast; much hotter and you start killing it, which means a slow or flat rise. The same goes for the melted butter.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note">
  **Why a skillet instead of the oven:** Cooking the muffins on a griddle browns the flat tops and bottoms while the sides stay pale and soft. That's the classic English muffin look.
</Aside>
