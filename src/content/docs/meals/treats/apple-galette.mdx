## Apple Galette

### Description

A galette is a free-form, single-crust "flat pie" — no pie pan, no blind baking, all rustic charm. Folding the crust up and over the filling gives it more crunchy edges than a regular pie.

- **Apple Cranberry** is based on the recipe by [ChefSteps](https://www.chefsteps.com/recipes/make-galette-not-pie): apples, cranberries, citrus zest, and warm spices — a great winter-fruit dessert for the Thanksgiving table. Swap in pears or quince for the apples, or swap the cranberries for raisins, dates, or another berry.
- **Apple Frangipane** is a house version built from two other recipes on this site: the [Buttery, Flaky Pie Crust](/meals/pantry/buttery-flaky-pie-crust-chefsteps/) (pâte brisée) and a small, apple-friendly batch of [Classic Frangipane](/meals/pantry/classic-frangipane-chefsteps/). Thin apple slices are fanned over the almond cream, with an optional layer of apple butter in between.

**Yield:** One 9-inch galette, 6–8 servings · **Total time:** 3½ hours (1½ hours active), plus making the dough

### Recipe

<Tabs syncKey="galette-version">
  <TabItem label="Apple Cranberry">

    <Steps>
    1. **Gather everything:** You'll need the pie dough very cold and rested — ideally made the day before, or pulled from a stash in the freezer.

    2. **Prep the fruit:** Use a melon baller to remove the stem, bottom, and core of each apple. Peel and slice into slender half-moons, about ⅛ inch thick. Place in a mixing bowl with the cranberries and zest the lemon and orange over the mix.

    3. **Season the filling:** In a small bowl, combine the brown sugar, salt, flour, malic acid, spices, and scraped vanilla seeds, rubbing the seeds in until dispersed. Toss onto the fruit gently until evenly coated. Try to keep the apple slices intact — it makes assembly easier.

    4. **Roll out the dough:** Have the filling nearby and a parchment-lined sheet pan ready — once the dough is rolled, assemble right away to keep it cold (if it warms up, chill it on the sheet pan before assembling). Lightly flour your table and press the dough round into a larger circle with your fingertips. Roll it out, rotating 45° between passes, until you have a solid 13-inch circle. Trim the edge with a knife or pizza roller for a cleaner bake (optional, cosmetic only).

    5. **Assemble:** Place the dough on the sheet pan and brush wash around the rim. Lay the apples in an even layer, forming a roughly 10-inch circle in the center and leaving a rim to fold over. Fold the edges over the filling one small section at a time, creating flaps as you go around, pressing each down gently. Dot the top of the filling with pats of the 30g of butter. Brush the folded edges with wash, including between the flaps, to help hold them in place. Sprinkle with coarse sugar if you like.

    6. **Chill:** Chill for at least 1 hour before baking — this lets the dough relax so it doesn't pull and shrink in the oven.

    7. **Bake:** Preheat the oven to 350°F (177°C). Bake on the center rack until the edges and bottom are deep golden brown and the filling is bubbling, about 60 minutes. A convection fan speeds browning; without one, it may take a little longer.

    8. **Cool:** Let the galette cool for at least 45 minutes before slicing.
    </Steps>

    <Aside type="note">
      **Why flour in the filling:** It binds the juice the fruit releases during baking. Pro bakeries use a modified food starch since it reheats better than cornstarch, but flour works fine here.
    </Aside>

  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Apple Frangipane">

    <Aside type="caution">
      **Work in progress:** This version is developed from the recipes above but hasn't been baked yet. Adjust the amounts, temperature, and timing after the first bake.
    </Aside>

    <Steps>
    1. **Make the dough ahead:** Make a batch of [pâte brisée](/meals/pantry/buttery-flaky-pie-crust-chefsteps/) through its step 5 (shaped into a disk and refrigerated for at least 4 hours), ideally the day before. Weigh out 400g for the galette.

    2. **Make the frangipane:** Cream the 55g of butter and 50g of sugar until pale and fluffy, about 3 minutes. Beat in the egg and almond flour in alternating additions, letting each fully incorporate. Mix in the 8g of flour, then the vanilla, almond extract, and salt. Leave it at soft room temperature so it spreads easily.

    3. **Prep the apples:** Core the apples (a melon baller works well) and peel them — or leave the skins on for color. Slice into ⅛-inch half-moons. Toss gently with the 25g of sugar, lemon juice, cinnamon, and a pinch of salt. Let sit for about 10 minutes, then **pour off the liquid** that collects — this keeps the frangipane from going soggy.

    4. **Roll out the dough:** On a lightly floured surface, roll the 400g of dough into a 13-inch circle, about 3mm thick, rotating 45° between passes. Transfer to a parchment-lined sheet pan.

    5. **Spread the frangipane:** Spread the frangipane in an even layer in a 9-inch circle in the center, leaving a 2-inch border of bare dough.

    6. **Add the apple butter (optional):** Spread the 60g of apple butter thinly over the frangipane with an offset spatula. Keep it a thin layer — too much makes the center wet and heavy.

    7. **Arrange the apples:** Shingle the apple slices in overlapping concentric circles over the frangipane, starting at the outer edge and working toward the center.

    8. **Fold and finish:** Fold the dough border up and over the edge of the filling one section at a time, pleating as you go and pressing each flap down gently. Brush the apples with the 20g of melted butter. Brush the crust with egg wash, including between the flaps, and sprinkle the crust with the turbinado sugar.

    9. **Chill:** Chill for at least 1 hour so the dough relaxes and doesn't shrink in the oven. Preheat the oven to 375°F (190°C) with a rack in the lower-middle position.

    10. **Bake:** Bake for 50–60 minutes, until the crust is deep golden brown (check the bottom), the frangipane is puffed and set between the apples, and the apple edges are lightly browned. Tent with foil if the apples darken too fast.

    11. **Glaze (optional):** Warm 20g of apple butter with a splash of water in a small saucepan until loose, then brush it over the hot apples for shine.

    12. **Cool:** Let the galette cool for at least 45 minutes so the frangipane sets before slicing.
    </Steps>

    <Aside type="note">
      **Why this frangipane:** ChefSteps' classic frangipane is almond-forward, which works for Galette des Rois but can drown out apples. This ¼ batch cuts the sugar about 10% (the apples and apple butter add sweetness) and uses half the almond extract, so it tastes nutty without the almond extract taking over. American-style frangipane (no pastry cream) is also sturdier and better at soaking up the apple juices. The recipe makes about 225g — enough for a thin, even layer.
    </Aside>

  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

<Aside type="tip">
  **Egg wash:** Whisk 1 egg, 1 egg yolk, a pinch of salt, and a splash of water (about 25ml), then strain for a smooth, lump-free wash. Keeps up to 2 days refrigerated. If avoiding eggs, evaporated milk or plain milk works well too — not quite as shiny, but it holds the flaps. A water-only wash is not recommended.
</Aside>
